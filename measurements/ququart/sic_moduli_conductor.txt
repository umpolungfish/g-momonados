1     Introduction: The Cycle
A symmetric informationally complete positive operator-valued measure (SIC-POVM) in dimension 𝑑 is a set
of 𝑑2 unit vectors in ℂ𝑑 with equal pairwise overlap |⟨𝜓𝑗 , 𝜓𝑘 ⟩|2 = 1/(𝑑 + 1) for 𝑗 ≠ 𝑘. Zauner conjectured
in 1999 that such configurations exist in every dimension, generated as orbits of a single fiducial under the
Weyl–Heisenberg group [1]. The conjecture remains open.
Appleby, Bengtsson, Flammia, McConnell, and Yard established that fiducial coordinates are algebraic numbers
in ray class fields of 𝐹 = ℚ(√𝑚𝑑 ), where 𝑚𝑑 is the squarefree part of (𝑑 + 1)(𝑑 − 3) [2, 3, 4]. This ties SIC
existence to Hilbert’s twelfth problem for real quadratic fields. But knowing the fiducial lives in some ray
class field is not knowing which. The conductor determines the ray class group, the character count, and
whether Stark machinery applies.
This paper determines the conductor for the moduli 𝑁𝑘 = |𝜓𝑘 |2 . But the determination is not linear. The
conductor formula, the Belnap trilattice, and the IMASM machine grammar are not three separate results
connected by interpretation. They are one structure expressing itself in three substrates: arithmetic, logical,
and operational.

2     The Moduli Field Is Not the Coordinate Field
The moduli 𝑁𝑘 = |𝜓𝑘 |2 are real at the fiducial embedding. The coordinates 𝜓𝑘 are complex. Reality at one
embedding does not imply total reality: the field can be, and is, complex at the conjugate place.

This distinction has practical teeth. PARI’s bnrstark computes Stark units for totally real class fields and
refuses anything else. An earlier draft claimed the moduli field was totally real, misled by an integer relation
fitting noise rather than recovering structure. The test that separates genuine relations from fitted noise is
coefficient height stability: a true algebraic relation maintains constant height as working precision rises; a
spurious fit sees height grow because each new digit demands new coefficients.
Proposition 2.1 (𝑑 = 12 moduli field). The twelve moduli of the 𝑑 = 12 fiducial generate a field of degree
sixteen over ℚ, signature (8, 4), abelian of degree eight over 𝐹 = ℚ(√13), with conductor 𝔭32 𝔭3 of norm 192,
exactly one infinite place ramified, ray class group (ℤ/2)3 of order eight, and trivial subgroup. The minimal
polynomial of 𝑁0 is 97344𝑥4 − 32448𝑥3 + 3224𝑥2 − 104𝑥 + 1.
The field is not totally real. The moduli field is the full ray class field at a modulus whose archimedean part
is one place: not none, not both. At 𝑑 = 12, 𝑁1 has a degree-eight minimal polynomial of height 33 bits,
stable at 600, 900, and 1300 digits. The primitive element ∑𝑘 𝑘𝑁𝑘 has degree sixteen at height 80 bits, likewise
stable. The retracted totally-real-octic claim returned heights of 177, 287, 435 bits at those precisions: the
signature of a fit, not a fact.
Every claim in this paper is stated at a precision the input supports. The exact coordinates are known to
roughly sixteen hundred digits; seeking relations beyond that fits digits that do not exist.


3     Method: Height Stability and Conductor Enumeration
The method is uniform across calibration dimensions:

    1. Take the fiducial to published precision. Form moduli 𝑁𝑘 = |𝜓𝑘 |2 .
    2. Seek minimal polynomials via integer relation against a power basis, including a primitive element
       ∑𝑘 𝑘𝑁𝑘 .
    3. Accept only if coefficient height is stable across at least three precisions spanning several hundred
       digits.
    4. Identify the field from the accepted polynomial; compute signature; verify abelian-ness over 𝐹 by
       checking degree divides ray class group order and Galois group matches.
    5. Enumerate moduli of 𝐹 of small norm; compute ray class groups with infinite places treated both ways;
       select the smallest modulus whose group has correct order, type, and trivial subgroup.

Two warnings, both learned: the height test is necessary, not decorative; and the archimedean part must be
searched, not assumed. Taking both infinite places unramified because the moduli are real is exactly the error
that produced the retracted claim.


4     Calibration Cross-Sections: 𝑑 = 4, 8, 12
Three dimensions have exact fiducials and class number one: 𝑑 = 4, 8, 12. They are not building blocks
leading to 𝑑 = 2048. They are cross-sections where the same cyclic structure becomes visible.
Dimension four has 𝑣2 (4) = 2, conductor exponent three. Dimension eight has 𝑣2 (8) = 3, exponent four.
Dimension twelve has 𝑣2 (12) = 2, exponent three, plus the prime above three since 3 ∣ 12.
Dimension eight refutes the naive constant-exponent guess. Exponent three fits 𝑑 = 4 and 𝑑 = 12 perfectly.
Only 𝑑 = 8 separates constant from 𝑣2 (𝑑) + 1, decisively.

The odd part carries subtlety. Three splits in ℚ(√13), so the 𝑑 = 12 conductor factor has norm three. Three
is inert in ℚ(√5) with norm nine. The odd contribution concerns primes of 𝐹 above rational primes dividing
𝑑, not rational integers themselves. The two cases would be indistinguishable with only one computation.
Three calibration points is the minimum, not a luxury.

8.3    The Operational Substrate: IMASM and sixteen3
The IMASM machine’s native alphabet is B4: four values {𝑁, 𝑇, 𝐹, 𝐵}, two bits each. A byte is four B4 cells;
